GenVo

Survive as one small creature on a living planet. Find a mate, raise your young, and see what your family becomes.

What it simulates: a planet's ecology and inheritance, for players who like to survive as one small creature and watch what their family becomes.

How it plays

You start as a single creature in a world already full of life. Plants grow, seed and spread. Other animals graze, hunt, court and die. Nothing waits for you.

  • Survive. Watch your food and water, rest, and keep out of a Stalker's sight. Dense plants hide you; sprinting costs breath.
  • Explore. Walk off the edge of the map into the next region of a persistent planet: wetlands, dry ground, coasts, snow and caves.
  • Find a mate. Courtship is a small exchange with another creature, and either of you can decline.
  • Raise your young. Pregnancy and birth draw on the parents' real reserves. Build a shelter, feed the young and teach them.
  • Pass it on. Offspring inherit body parts and traits from both parents, with small variations. When one grows up, you continue as them.

Your body decides what you can do. Legs set your speed, eyes your sight, your mouth what you can eat; wings, gills and digging limbs open other ways to live. In the Creature Lab you can shape a starting creature, or the next generation when the chance comes.

Why it's a simulation

The world runs on rules, not scripts. Rain soaks into soil, fills pools and drains away in rivers. Seasons bring snow and drought. Plants need light, water and minerals, and grazing animals return those minerals where they feed. Food is finite and shared. Populations grow, shrink and move, and some disappear. Your lineage survives or fails inside that system.

What's here now

Playable: survival, feeding and drinking, shelter, courtship, pregnancy and birth, raising young, inheritance, handing control to a descendant, the Creature Lab, and travel between planetary regions with seasons and weather. The game saves in your browser; choose Continue to pick up where you left off.

Still developing: long-run evolution (new species emerge only over many generations), some advanced ecology, and performance in very old worlds on slower machines. Touch play works, but GenVo is best with a keyboard and mouse.

Controls

  • Move: WASD or arrow keys; hold Shift to sprint
  • Interact / eat: E (click food or a creature to see its actions)
  • Drink: Shift+E near water
  • Attack: Space; hold J to brace against a charge
  • Field guide: Y. Journal: L. Help: ?
  • Family: Tab switches to another family member; H hands control to a grown descendant
  • Speed: [ and ] for 2x and 4x (your creature acts on its own); P pauses

The tutorial walks you through food, water and finding a mate.
