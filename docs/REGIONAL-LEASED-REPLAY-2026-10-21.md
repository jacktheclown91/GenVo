# Canonical replay from leased regional records — October 21, 2026

`RegionalCheckpointReader.replayNext()` now executes the oldest due canonical regional stride directly from persisted records. It plans from the captured directory, acquires the active record and one due session, stages real Simulation ticks with the directory's global identity watermark, then atomically publishes the changed session and coupled active allocation headers. Unrelated archive/survey payloads remain on disk. The operation advances neither the active clock nor its animals, resources or RNG. It uses the saved scheduling policy and life-stride bound; it does not manufacture populations, environmental catch-up or background elapsed time.

Resident `advanceRemoteBiology` and leased replay share `stageRemoteBiology`, extracted from the existing tick/snapshot/ledger kernel. The stage restores the detached remote Simulation, runs its original ticks, validates daily water/salt receipts, population bounds, exact tick consumption, biological/environmental ledgers and local animal ownership, and returns a candidate without publishing to its input owner. Resident publication retains its full cross-region ownership check and dirty-generation hook. Leased publication checks candidate identities against the full persisted directory through the existing atomic update codec. Both update global allocation watermarks exactly as before.

Replay requires an exclusive reader with two available lease slots. Concurrent acquisitions or another replay on that reader reject. A completed or failed replay closes the reader and releases both payload references; the caller opens a fresh captured reader for the next job. A no-work result returns null without loading a payload. Closing before publication prevents admission; once the IndexedDB write is enqueued it follows that transaction's atomic commit/abort outcome, rather than treating lease release as rollback. The temporary remote Simulation and candidate are transient additional copies within those two regions: this is a bound on decoded region ownership, not a claim of only two array allocations or measured total peak bytes.
