# Yields from a physical basis (October 23, 2026)

**Creator request:** recalibrate yields from a physical basis (option 1 of [the first-month study](WILDLIFE-NEEDS-2026-10-23.md)) as a separate measured step, after [option 2](DAY-ACTIVITY-2026-10-23.md).

## Checking each yield against physical figures

The game's mass unit is taken as roughly 1 kg: the species weigh 0.68–2.1. Energy units are each species' own. Real needs fix a resting day at maxEnergy/15 units, and Kleiber's law puts a resting day at about 70 × M^0.75 kcal, which gives the energy units per kcal for that species.

| Yield | Game value | Physical estimate | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| Plant food per unit (if a unit is about 1 kg fresh on a 1 m² tile) | bramble 57.6, sunmoss 25 | fruit about 55 (0.7 kcal/g, 80% assimilated); moss about 27, grass about 45 | consistent |
| Plant growth | sunmoss up to about 19 g fresh per tile per day | 1.4 kg dry per m² a year, productive grassland | consistent |
| Food per bite | a resting mosskip needs about one 220 g bite a day | about 27% of body mass of fresh forage, normal for a small herbivore | consistent |
| **Animal tissue** (prey, carrion, aquatic larvae, fry, detritus) | **34 per unit** | **99–139 per unit** (about 1.5 kcal/g carcass, 85% assimilated) | **about three to four times too low** |

## What was built: `tissue-energy/1`, opt-in, active only with real needs

`src/ecology/tissue-energy.js` is saved with each world and copied on crossings.

- **Tissue energy in the eater's own units:** 1,500 kcal per unit of mass, times the species' units per kcal, times 0.85 assimilation for an eater. By species: mosskip 126, rootling 112, needlejaw 99, rillsifter 139, mirelick 126, podshell 106, bankjaw 115, GenVo 114. At rest every species then needs 4.6–6% of its body mass in meat a day; small carnivores in the wild eat about 5–10%.
- **One kill meal** is at most what fills the eater's reserves and at most a gorge of 20% of its body mass. The rest stays on the carcass as carrion. Recorded worlds eat the whole body at the kill and lose what does not fit.
- **One rule for wildlife and player:** kills, wound bites, carrion bites, the player's kill and Eat.
- **Aquatic foods that are animal tissue** (larvae, fry, detritus and marine consumers) count the same way. Plant-like aquatic foods are unchanged.
- **Growing and repairing tissue** (juvenile growth, healing repair, regrowing wounded tissue) cost the same tissue energy.
- **Measurement switch:** `sim.correctionExperiment.tissueGrowth=false` (development only) keeps the recorded growth and repair cost for comparison.
- **Small shared-file change:** `ui/journal.js` and `ui/body-cost-reading.js` pass the world's tissue energy, so the body-cost reading stays truthful.

**Test:** `tests/tissue-energy.mjs` (gate) checks that:
- every species needs 3.5–7.5% of its body mass in meat a day at rest;
- recorded worlds keep 34;
- a kill leaves the uneaten carcass as carrion, while recorded worlds eat the whole body;
- the meal is limited by the gorge when hungry and by reserves when nearly full;
- growth costs the tissue energy;
- saves keep the policy.

## What it shows

- **Meat energy does what physics says.** Predation halves on every seed, because hunters need about a third as many kills. Hunters persist far longer, and in the full arm to the end of the year.
- **Physical growth cost conflicts with compressed development.**
  - Per gram it is right. But a mosskip matures in 20 days, while real animals its size take months, so a day of growth costs several times what it would in nature.
  - Young starvation triples: 7 to 31 on GENVO8-TEST-A, and 10 to 27 on PLANET-OBS-2 against the recorded growth cost.
  - Keeping the recorded growth cost avoids that, but then growing tissue costs a quarter of what eating it yields.
- **Wild herbivores still die out within about 90–180 days, and the cause is now clear** (`tools/probe-basal-mass.mjs`):
  - founding wild bodies weigh about 1.6× (mosskip) and 1.3× (rootling) their species' reference mass;
  - resting costs rise with actual body mass, but real needs were calibrated on the reference body, and the reserve (`maxEnergy`) does not grow with body size;
  - so a typical wild mosskip spends 0.109 of its reserve a day at rest, against the design's 0.067. It lasts about 9 days, not the approved 14–21, and needs about 1.6 times the food the calibration assumed.
  - In real animals heavier individuals carry proportionally larger reserves, and their metabolism grows more slowly than their mass (about mass^0.75), so they last longer, not shorter.
- **The founding and breeding-support estimates still compute needs on the old metabolism** (`founderDailyNeed` in `resource-founding.js`, used by `breeding-support.js`). They believe animals need about a fifth of what real needs require, which is likely why worlds are founded, and allowed to breed, beyond what they can carry. These estimates were left unchanged in this step.

## Decisions needed

1. **Growth and repair cost under tissue energy:** physical (young starve on compressed development), or recorded (tissue cheaper to grow than to eat)?
2. **Body size and reserves under real needs.** The approved target was 14–21 days of food for every organism at rest; wild bodies actually get about 9. Options:
   - **(a)** reserves scale with body mass and resting cost with mass^0.75, physically faithful: heavier bodies last longer, and heavy anatomy still costs more food per day;
   - **(b)** calibrate each animal's resting cost to its own body, so every animal lasts 15 days, and anatomy no longer changes reserve days;
   - **(c)** keep it as it is.
3. **Founding and breeding-support estimates** computing needs under the real-needs rules, which is option 4 (densities).

Tissue energy stays opt-in alongside the other v1 rules until these are settled.
