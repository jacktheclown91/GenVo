# First-generation vertical slice contract | October 21, 2026

This is the acceptance direction for the next playable GenVo milestone. It is **not** a declaration that the full game, first-generation experience or underlying ecology is finished. The one canonical Creature-stage design and [implementation authorities](../design/governance/implementation-authorities.json) still govern every subsystem.

## Player identity (critical gate, first 5–10 minutes)

Default to **one embodied, vulnerable creature carrying a continuing lineage**. It is neither an omniscient world observer nor a godlike family director. Relatives retain their own autonomy. The active organism can eat, drink, court, physically interact, explore and die; its choices affect the young, the local environment and which compatible descendant may be played next. On a real first-generation handoff, show its *actual* surviving heir, inherited changes and a credible stake in what was lost or retained. Family-level guidance should only emerge when the lineage grows and the relevant capacity actually exists.

Opening play must earn this conclusion through the creature's visible actions and consequences, not primarily a lore briefing, mission text or fabricated scripted danger. Observe a new player with no access to developer panels: can they identify the controlled creature, its immediate needs, and why surviving to a next generation matters?

## Anatomy must change verbs, not merely statistics (critical gate)

Create a deliberately reachable fresh-world path and several valid founder/scenario alternatives to four **existing physical actions** (for example a real paired spring jump, functional climbing, true excavating/burrowing, and finite-height gliding or supported diving). Each must:
- Be accessible on actual legal inherited/buildable anatomy within the first-generation test, not granted for free in a deceptive tutorial.
- Have a valid local opportunity, real cost, clear mouse-first affordance, visibly different animation/physics and a recognizable obstacle or reward.
- Report genuine blockers (insufficient organs, energy, depth, supporting tree, elevation, passable substrate) without hidden-coordinate leakage.
- Work in native browser play. No action is counted solely because a UI button exists or a controlled unit-test succeeds.

## One generation of ecological consequences (high)

Capture **one or two naturally generated or seeded-from-real-ecology** pressure changes that alter the local decision: loss of reliable water, predator near brood/home, flood/fire, or temperature relief through real anatomy and shelter. A curated immutable scenario may start at a recorded real condition; do not conjure special event spawns, teleport predators or bypass mass/water accounting to stage drama. The player must have more than one meaningful response, including retreat, adaptation of behavior or migration, and the outcome must persist.

## Performance, reliability, visuals

- Run a continuously recorded **20–40 minute** single-region player session in actual Chromium; pursue a stable 30+ fps with non-jarring frame-time distribution. Disclose stalls, frame gaps, browser exceptions and machine/context. If multiple regions are tested, report them separately. Do not solve stalls by dropping actual organisms, skipping ecology or lowering tick cadence.
- New World using current rules and Continue are the default, comprehensible save loop. Legacy physics adoption and backup recovery remain available from advanced tools, never forced into the opening route. Verify exact continue of anatomy, RNG, offspring, resources and ecological event history.
- At normal top-down camera scale, the active body silhouette, its functional organs, injury/breath/thermal state and one important nearby threat/resource should be interpretable by sight or restrained cues. No unearned omniscient sensing or unrelated HUD flood.
- A bounded local region needs actual edible diversity, recognizable competitor or predator, and meaningful habitat variation. Measure encounter opportunities in natural play, not just catalog size.

## Cohesive families without hard species rules

The experimental `local-visual-assortment/1` branch biases **inherited mate preferences**, displays and selection toward real pigment and mounted silhouette while maintaining reciprocal consent, hybrids and negative contrast tastes. The current .25 body-plan mutation chance is **not changed by this pass**. Before modifying it, compare controlled matched cohorts and independent 30–60 day natural runs for:
- Within-locality hue/build dispersion and between-locality separation.
- Actual reciprocal mating decisions, distances, hybrid births and compatible mate availability.
- Lineage survival, fertility/inbreeding effects, allele diversity and rare exceptions.
- Restored historical seeds/state versus versioned new worlds.

No universal fixed species body, mandatory local mate, same-colour gate, guaranteed reproduction, arbitrary species cap or forced offspring visual outcome.

## Integration order for Codex

1. Integrate the separately verified sensory/cave PR on canonical local source with review of in-flight work.
2. Review and merge the versioned mate-assortment experiment **only if** its contracts and direct visual observations agree. Historical policy correctness is mandatory.
3. Build and author one recognizable first-generation arc. Make anatomy→verb opportunities and an actual consequential handoff happen in a reachable prepared world.
4. Observe creator and new-player behavior and real frame pacing, then repair barriers one by one.
5. Tune local assortment, mutation and pressure pacing using distinct controlled and natural evidence. Heavy 100-day full-system campaigns come after the playable mechanics and render polish are ready.

**Exit criteria:** A novice knows which body they control, exercises at least three genuinely distinct anatomical verbs in the first generation, faces a readable ecological choice, and reaches or clearly anticipates an authentic heir handoff without developer panels, policy friction or recurring disruptive stalls.
